No coding required for this paper. RDA 112P tests your ability to design and build working hardware solutions using discrete electronics. Option A — Logic Gate / Conditional Algorithm (60 marks)
Task 01
Design a Combinational Logic Circuit
60 marks

Using discrete components from the pool — switches, relays, transistors, LEDs, LDRs, or a combination — design and build a hardware circuit that implements a meaningful conditional logic statement of the form:

IF [event A] AND/OR [event B] → THEN [output C]

Your circuit must respond to real physical inputs (e.g. switches pressed by hand, LDR responding to light, relay activated by a voltage threshold) and produce a visible or audible output (e.g. LED lights, relay clicks, buzzer sounds). The more conditions and outputs your logic handles, the higher your score.

Example Ideas (pick your own)
  • AND Gate simulation: LED lights only when Switch A AND Switch B are both pressed.
  • OR + NOT logic: Relay activates when either Switch A OR Switch B is pressed, but NOT when both are pressed simultaneously (XOR).
  • Light-controlled relay: LDR detects darkness → transistor conducts → relay switches → LED turns on. Add a manual override switch for bonus complexity.
  • Two-input decision circuit: Two LDRs control two separate relay paths — if both LDRs are in shadow, a third "alarm" LED activates.
  • Multi-stage cascade: Output of one relay becomes input to the next — implement a 3-state logic chain.
Marking Rubric
Circuit diagram submitted and legible10 marks
Circuit built correctly and neatly15 marks
Logic function works as described20 marks
Complexity of logic (conditions, stages)10 marks
Multimeter verification of voltages / correct values5 marks
Option B — Battery Level Indicator (60 marks)
Task 02
Build a Multi-Level Battery / Voltage Indicator
60 marks

Using the variable bench power supply to simulate a battery at different voltage levels, design and build a circuit that visually indicates the charge level across multiple defined thresholds. Your circuit must light different LEDs (or drive relays) depending on the input voltage.

Target at least three distinct levels (e.g. full → medium → low → critical), each triggering a different LED or combination. Use resistors and transistors (or comparators if available) to set your voltage thresholds accurately.

  • Level 1 — Full (e.g. 11–12V): Green LED on, all others off.
  • Level 2 — Medium (e.g. 8–10V): Yellow LED on, red and green off.
  • Level 3 — Low (e.g. 5–7V): Red LED on.
  • Level 4 — Critical (<5V): Red LED flashes or relay activates (optional for bonus marks).
  • Use the multimeter to verify each threshold voltage and show the supervisor.
Marking Rubric
Circuit diagram submitted and legible10 marks
At least 3 levels correctly indicated by LEDs20 marks
Voltage thresholds accurate (verified by multimeter)15 marks
Circuit neatness and component arrangement10 marks
Bonus: 4th level with flashing/relay output+5 bonus
Mandatory for All Students (40 marks)
All Options
Documentation & Demonstration Requirements
40 marks
  • Live Demonstration (20 marks): Circuit must be demonstrated to a supervisor in the lab. The supervisor will test your inputs and verify outputs respond correctly. You must be able to explain your design choices verbally.
  • Video Evidence (10 marks): A video of the working circuit must be recorded before leaving the lab. Show all inputs being toggled and outputs responding. This is used for any future grade disputes.
  • Circuit Diagram (10 marks): Drawn on paper, labelled with component values and pin connections. Submitted to supervisor at end of exam.
Submission & Video Instructions

After your live demonstration is scored by the supervisor, ensure:
1. A video of your working circuit has been recorded (supervisor or lab assistant assists if needed).
2. Your circuit diagram is handed to the supervisor before leaving.
3. No components are to be removed from lab equipment without sign-off.

There is no email submission for RDA 112P — this is a fully hardware practical. All grading happens live in the lab.
