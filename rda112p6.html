Examination Brief

A soldering station, digital multimeter, and variable DC power supply are provided. Your circuit must be demonstrated live to a supervisor for immediate scoring. Option A — Logic Gate / Conditional Circuit
Option A
Design a Combinational Logic Circuit
30 marks

Using discrete components from the pool — switches, relays, transistors, LEDs, LDRs, or any combination — design and build a circuit that implements a meaningful conditional logic statement of the form:

IF [event A] AND / OR [event B] → THEN [output C]

Your circuit must respond to real physical inputs and produce a visible output. The more conditions your logic handles, the stronger your solution. You are free to design any circuit that demonstrates clear conditional logic — do not copy a textbook; show your thinking.

Example ideas — invent your own
  • AND gate: LED lights only when Switch A AND Switch B are both pressed simultaneously.
  • XOR logic: Relay activates when Switch A OR Switch B is pressed, but NOT when both are pressed at the same time.
  • Light-controlled relay: LDR detects darkness → transistor conducts → relay activates → LED on. Add a manual override switch for extra complexity.
  • Dual LDR decision: Both LDRs in shadow → alarm LED activates. One in light → normal state.
  • Cascaded relay logic: Output of one relay becomes input trigger for the next — a 3-stage logic chain.
Option B — Battery Level Indicator
Option B
Build a Multi-Level Voltage / Battery Indicator
30 marks

Using the variable bench power supply to simulate a battery at different charge states, design and build a circuit that visually indicates the voltage level across at least three defined thresholds — each threshold lighting a different LED or combination of LEDs.

Use resistors and transistors to define your voltage thresholds accurately. Verify each threshold with the multimeter during your demonstration.

  • Level 1 — Full (e.g. 11–12 V): Green LED on, others off.
  • Level 2 — Medium (e.g. 7–10 V): Yellow LED on.
  • Level 3 — Low (e.g. 4–6 V): Red LED on.
  • Level 4 — Critical (below 4 V) — Optional bonus: Red LED flashes or relay triggers a warning output.
  • Demonstrate each level to the supervisor by adjusting the PSU and confirming the correct LED responds.
Demonstration & Evidence Requirements

1. Live demo to supervisor — supervisor will test your inputs and verify outputs. Be ready to explain your design choices verbally.
2. Video recording — record a clear video of your circuit working (inputs toggled, correct outputs responding) before leaving the lab. This is required for any future grade inquiry.
3. Circuit diagram — hand your labelled paper sketch to the supervisor before leaving.
