Keynote Address · ThinkUP Innovation Hub Launch

Think UP.
Own UP. Build.

An address to the next generation of Nigerian builders

Dr. Olusola Sayeed Ayoola, PhD Founder & CEO, RAIN Ibadan · 11 July 2026

↓ Read the speech

Opening

Set the Room

Protocols.

I want to start by saying thank you to my brother 'Niyi Kolade — a man who didn't just dream, he built. What you see around you today is a statement. It is a declaration. ThinkUP Innovation Hub is proof that when one person refuses to wait, something real happens.

And I am honoured to be here with us all, celebrating this significant milestone today.

My job here is simple — to kindle a spark in the minds of our young compatriots who are at the heart of this great investment.

So let's go.

Part One

The Problem

It's not a problem to have problems. It's a problem to do nothing about them.

Nigeria has problems. Big ones. Unemployment. Inflation. Infrastructure that frustrates. A government that sometimes feels far away. Young people with degrees and no jobs. Young people with ideas and no support. Young people with fire — and nowhere to burn.

Every nation on earth has had its season of struggle. Every great economy you admire today has had its darkest hour. America had a civil war. China had a cultural revolution. South Korea was once poorer than Nigeria. Singapore was a swamp.

The real problem — the dangerous problem — is when you have a problem and you do nothing about it.

There is a cycle that has repeated itself throughout human history. Listen carefully:

Strong men build strong economies. Strong economies produce comfort. Comfort produces weak men. Weak men produce weak economies. And weak economies? They produce strong men again.

And the cycle continues.

So where are we in the cycle? We are at the bottom of the clock. It is six o'clock. And some of us are afraid. Some of us are discouraged. Some of us have started to believe that Nigeria is finished.

But a clock that reaches six o'clock doesn't stop. It keeps moving. It goes back up to twelve.

  • The sun sets — but it rises again.
  • What falls from the sky evaporates and comes back as rain.
  • What goes around, comes around.

We have not committed a crime. We have not sinned. God is simply giving us an opportunity — to rekindle, to rebuild, to redevelop. To prove what we are made of.

Own it. Don't be ashamed of it.

Part Two

The Hope

If there was no opportunity here, why are they coming?

Now let me show you something.

Why are Chinese businessmen flooding Nigerian markets? Why are Indian companies setting up here? Why are multinationals that left during the hardship — quietly coming back?

They are not coming because Nigeria is finished. They are coming because they can see what some of us have stopped seeing.

Opportunity.

When there is a large population, there is a market. When there is a gap, there is a business. When things are broken, there is someone who will be paid to fix them.

Yes, things are expensive. Yes, things are hard. But let me tell you what matters more than price — stability. Can I start a business today and estimate what my profit will be next month? Can I plan? Can I project? When there is stability, even imperfect stability, businesses survive.

And now I ask you — can you start something?

  • Can you repair something?
  • Can you sell something?
  • Can you build something?
  • Can you teach something?
  • Can you service something?
  • Can you code? Can you design?

Because if you can do any one of those things — you have a business.

The only thing stopping most people is not capacity. It is not even capital. It is environment. Lack of positive vibes. It is having nowhere to go. Nowhere to be serious. Nowhere to leave your house and say — today, I am building something. A saner clime where amenities work.

That is what ThinkUP has done. That is why we are here today.

Part Three

The Mindset Shift

The idea was always inside you. You just needed the right environment to see it.

I want to talk about something that many people don't like to admit.

Have you noticed what happens when a Nigerian travels abroad? Something changes.

The same person who said "nothing works in Nigeria" — lands in London, or Toronto, or Dubai — and suddenly, ideas start flowing. They walk into a mall and say "we don't have this at home." They ride a clean bus and say "why can't we do this?" They see a system working and say "I could set this up back home."

And some of them do. And it works.

So what happened? Did they become smarter? Did they get a new brain on the plane? No.

The environment spoke to them. The orderliness. The structure. The possibility in the air. It unlocked something that was already inside them.

This is what a pessimistic, sadistic, hopeless environment does to a people — it shuts the imagination down. It tells you "don't bother." It tells you "it's not possible." It whispers "who are you to try?"

But this — what 'Niyi has built here — is an environment that says something different.

This environment says: think. This environment says: try. This environment says: you belong here. Your idea is valid. Your hustle is honourable. Come. Sit down. Build.

We need more of this. Not just in Ibadan. Across Nigeria. Because the ideas are in us. They have always been in us. We just need places that call them out.

Part Four

We Can Do

Never wait for government. Let government meet you at it.

Let me tell you a short story.

When I started RAIN — Robotics and Artificial Intelligence Nigeria — people thought I was dreaming. Robotics? In Nigeria? AI? Here?

The name itself was a statement of faith. RAIN. Because rain falls on dry ground. Rain comes when things look dead. Rain is what makes things grow.

Government was not forthcoming. Funding was not easy. The ecosystem was not ready.

But the passion — the passion didn't depend on government. It depended on the people.

On young Nigerians who wanted to learn. Who wanted to build. Who showed up. Who sacrificed. Who believed.

And today, RAIN is real. The trainees are real. The projects and partners are real. The impact is real.

This is happening across Nigeria. Young people in Lagos, Abuja, Ibadan, Kano — building, coding, farming with technology, creating content, opening businesses out of their bedrooms, out of co-working spaces, out of faith.

The movement is already happening. We just need to own it.

Part Five

Stop Complaining. Come With a Plan.

Stop waiting. Start planting.

Let me speak directly to the young people in this room.

There is a farmer. He has a seed. The ground looks dry. The sky looks empty. Rain seems far away.

If he loses hope — he will not plant. And nothing will grow.

But if he plants — even in dry ground — something begins. The seed goes into the soil. It looks like burial. It looks like loss. It looks like death.

But it is not death. It is the beginning of life.

Something has to decay before it grows. That is not failure — that is process.

  • You have to crawl before you can walk.
  • You have to walk before you can run.
  • You have to run before you can fly.

This is life. Own it.

So what must you own?

Own your skills.

The world is paying for competence. Not certificates — competence. Learn something real. Master it. Offer it.

Own your identity.

You are Nigerian. That is not a disadvantage — it is a superpower, if you choose to use it. We are creative, resilient, resourceful people. The world knows this. We must know it about ourselves.

Own your narrative.

Stop repeating that Nigeria is hopeless. Your words are programming your mind. Speak what you want to build. Speak what you believe is possible.

Own your future.

Stop waiting for government to save you. Stop waiting for the perfect condition. The condition will never be perfect. Plant now. Water now. Build now.

We are great people. We just have not had enough resources. But we can be the greatest resource — if we learn to share, if we learn to co-create, if we learn to cohabit, if we learn to build together.

Closing

ThinkUP. WiseUP. OwnUP. Build.
  • Don't just attend the hub. Use it.
  • Don't just take the training. Apply it.
  • Don't just dream the dream. Do the work.

Think UP

…Innovate. Let your mind go to places it has never been.

Wise UP

Learn. Grow. Stop making excuses.

Own UP

Take responsibility for your life, your community, your country.

Build

Nigeria is not finished. Nigeria is just getting started.

And Ibadan — the city of innovation, the city of history, the city of resilience — Ibadan will lead the way. We must therefore hold it dear and do our best to make it happen in our time.

Thank you. God bless you. God bless Nigeria.

And congratulations to ThinkUP Innovation Hub — may you never stop rising.
