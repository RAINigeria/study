**Keynote Address — Dr. Olusola Ayoola**
26th International Conference on Mathematical Sciences and Optimization
Theme: *From Theory to Impact — Mathematical Sciences, Optimization and AI in National Development*

## ▶ SLIDE 1 — TITLE

All protocols duly observed.

Distinguished ladies and gentlemen, good morning.

Let me thank the organizers of this conference for the honour of standing before you this morning. And let me say straight away that your theme this year — *From Theory to Impact* — is the correct theme. Not a fashionable theme. The **correct** theme. Because impact is exactly what is missing, and we are going to talk about it today.

My topic is the impact of mathematical sciences on nation building.

But before we enter that topic, come with me. Let us go back small.

*(Cue slide 2)*

---

## ▶ SLIDE 2 — THEY DID IT BY HAND

Look at this slide.

Geometry. Trigonometry. Calculus. Differential equations. Probability. Logarithms.

Now I want you to remember something about the people who built these things. They had no computer. They had no calculator. Many of them had no electricity in the room where they were working. What did they have? Paper. Pen. Candlelight. And a stubborn mind.

Let me give you one example, and I want it to enter your spirit.

Johannes Kepler wanted to know the true shape of the orbit of Mars. One planet. Just one. Do you know how long it took him? **Years.** Something like nine hundred pages of calculation, done by hand — and if he made one small error in the middle, he would go back and start again.

Years of a man's life. For one planet. And what did he give the world when he finished? The laws of planetary motion — the foundation on which, three hundred years later, we would put a satellite in the sky.

If you were Kepler, would you not be tired? Would you not say, "Ah, this thing is too hard, let somebody else do it"? But he did not stop. And because he did not stop, look at what we inherited.

*(Cue slide 3)*

---

## ▶ SLIDE 3 — FOUR EQUATIONS. ONE MODERN WORLD.

Let me show you what that hand-calculation built.

A man called James Clerk Maxwell sat down and wrote **four equations**. Four. Just four equations describing how electricity and magnetism behave.

And from those four equations, what came out? Radio came out. Television came out. Radar came out. Satellites came out. The mobile phone that is in your pocket right now, that you were pressing before you entered this hall — it is standing on those four equations.

Look at the rest of the list.

The aeroplane. Human beings looked at a bird and said "we want that." But wanting was not enough. It took fluid dynamics and calculus before that heavy iron could leave the ground. Mathematics is what lifted it.

Radioactivity — exponential decay, the half-life. That is how we understood the atom, and it is the same mathematics that today treats cancer in a hospital.

Modern medicine. Do you know why an MRI can look inside your body without cutting you? Fourier transforms. Mathematics from the eighteen hundreds is the reason your doctor can see your brain today.

> ⏱ **[CUT-1 — drop if short on time]**
> Global mobility — trigonometry and orbital mechanics. That is how GPS knows where you are standing. And insurance and pensions — probability. Somebody worked out how to put a number on risk, and that is how a nation learned to carry the burden of accident and of old age.

Distinguished ladies and gentlemen, everything we call modern life was built on mathematics done slowly, painfully, by hand.

*(Cue slide 4)*

---

## ▶ SLIDE 4 — SO WHAT IS OUR OWN EXCUSE?

So now let me ask you a question. And I want each one of us to answer it quietly, inside our own mind.

**They had nothing, and they built everything. So what is our own excuse?**

Because today, the ordinary laptop on your desk will solve in one second the problem that took Kepler four years. One second! The mathematics that Newton could not attempt because his hand would be tired — we can now run it ten thousand times before breakfast.

So tell me. Somebody should tell me.

**Should our children still be dying from diseases we already know how to cure?**

**Should our people still be hungry, in a country with this much land, this much rain, this much sun?**

**Should our grid still be collapsing in the year 2026?**

**Should we still be planning a whole nation on guesswork?**

Come on! It cannot be. It should not be.

We are not short of intelligence in this country. We are not short of brilliant people — half of them are sitting in this hall this morning, and the other half we have exported abroad. We are not short of the mathematics. The mathematics is already done. It is published. Much of it is free.

So what are we doing?

**That is why this topic matters.** That is the whole question I came here to put before you: can we take the mathematics we already have, and the tools that exist today, and use them to actually build this nation?

Let us now enter the matter properly.

*(Cue slide 5)*

## ▶ SLIDE 5 — THE BOTTLENECK HAS MOVED

Here is the most important shift of our lifetime, and I want you to hear it clearly.

For the whole of human history, mathematics was limited by the human being holding the pencil. You could imagine a system of ten thousand equations, but you could not solve it, because you would die first. So we simplified. We made every model small enough for a man to hold in his hand.

That limit is finished.

Look at the two boxes. Before, the question was: **can we compute it?** The machine was the bottleneck. Today the question is: **will we apply it?** The bottleneck has moved from the machine to the mind. From the processor to the policy.

So if a nation is still poor and still disorganised in the year 2026, "the problem is too complex" is no longer an acceptable answer. That excuse has expired. Today it is a **choice**.

*(Cue slide 6)*

## ▶ SLIDE 6 — WHAT IS NATION BUILDING?

Now, we say "nation building" every day. Every budget speech. Every campaign. Every commissioning ceremony. The words have become smooth, like a coin that has passed through too many hands. Let me give them back their edges.

Nation building is **not** concrete. If pouring concrete were enough, we would already be a developed nation — because we have poured plenty of concrete in this country. Some of it twice, in the same place, in the same year.

Nation building is the deliberate building of the systems, institutions, identity and capabilities by which a people becomes a functioning, self-sustaining, dignified nation.

But we are mathematicians here, so let me say it in our own language.

**Nation building is moving a nation from a local maximum to the global maximum.**

And there, ladies and gentlemen, is the whole story of our country in one sentence.

Because a local maximum is a very, very comfortable place. From where you are standing on that small hill, every single direction looks like a step down. Every reform looks like a loss. Every change looks like a risk. So what do you do? You stay there. And you give it a nice name. You call it stability.

**But a local maximum is a comfortable place to die.**

Nation building is having the courage to come down from that small comfortable hill, so that you can climb the real mountain.

*(Cue slide 7)*

## ▶ SLIDE 7 — NOT A SCALAR

> ⏱ **[CUT-2 — the whole of Slide 7 can be skipped if running long. Jump straight from Slide 6 to Slide 8.]**

And notice one more thing.

Nation building is not a scalar. It is a **vector**.

A scalar has only magnitude. Size. Quantity. Look at the left side — plenty people, plenty resources, plenty noise. We have all of that. Two hundred million plus. Oil. Gas. Solid minerals. Land. Sunshine. Noise — ah, we have serious magnitude in noise.

But a vector has magnitude **and direction**.

Many nations have magnitude. Very few have direction.

We are a large nation moving very fast. The only small problem is that we are not always sure where we are going.

**We have magnitude in abundance. What we lack is direction.**

*(Cue slide 8)*

## ▶ SLIDE 8 — WHOSE HEADACHE IS IT?

Now let us ask the uncomfortable question. Whose headache is this thing?

Is it government's headache? Partly. But if nation building were only a government function, then every country with a government would be built by now — and we know that is not true.

Is it the international community's headache? Let me be very direct this morning. No nation in the history of this world has ever been developed by foreigners. Foreign aid is a boundary condition. It is not the differential equation.

Is it the elders' headache? They set the initial conditions — but many of them will not be here for the solution. Is it the youth's headache? They will inherit the answer whether or not they were allowed to do the working.

So here is the correct answer, mathematically.

**It is a coupled system.** Every single one of us is a variable sitting inside somebody else's equation. There is no term you can set to zero without changing the whole solution. The teacher. The trader. The customs officer. The professor. The student. The man who inflates the contract. The woman who reports him. Every one of us is a coefficient.

So the question is not *whose* headache it is. The question is: **what is your coefficient, and what is its sign?** Positive, negative — or, most commonly in this country, **zero**.

And now permit me to offend my own people small.

A great deal of Nigerian academic mathematics is a magnificent contribution of exactly zero. Beautiful papers. Rigorously peer-reviewed. Published in good journals. Read by nobody who has ever signed a budget. We optimised for publication. We did not optimise for impact.

And that is exactly why your theme this year, *From Theory to Impact*, is not decoration. It is an accusation — and a correct one.

*(Cue slide 9)*

## ▶ SLIDE 9 — NIGERIA TODAY

Let us now look at ourselves honestly for one minute.

The World Bank estimates that ten million more Nigerians fell into extreme poverty in 2025, bringing us to roughly fifty-one percent below the international poverty line. Half of us.

The IMF recorded average inflation of about thirty-one percent in 2024, easing to around twenty-four percent by early 2025. Our infrastructure deficit is put at more than one hundred billion dollars.

And on the harmonised learning scale, where three hundred is the bare minimum and six hundred and twenty-five is advanced, our students score about three hundred and nine. We are barely clearing the floor. And where are our children failing hardest? **Mathematics.** So the very pipeline that should produce the people who will fix all of this — that pipeline is itself broken.

Let me add one more that is not on the slide, because it pains me the most. **We have not conducted a census since 2006.**

Twenty years of planning a whole country on an estimate. And everybody in this room knows the first law of applied mathematics: you cannot optimise what you have not measured. We are running a two-hundred-million-person nation on numbers we last verified when some of the students in this hall were in primary school.

*(Cue slide 10)*

## ▶ SLIDE 10 — THE PLAN CANNOT DELIVER

Now, this slide here is my favourite. And I want the room to pay attention, because this is the moment where mathematics stops being theory and starts being evidence.

Somebody actually did the mathematics on our national plan.

A published study applied multi-objective optimisation to Nigeria's Sustainable Development Goals under Agenda 2030. And the result was this: taken individually, the goals were achievable at eighty-one percent, eighty-three percent, and eighty-nine percent. Very respectable.

But when you put them together in one system — the overall achievement function could only reach about **seventy-three percent**.

Do you understand what that is telling us?

It is telling us the goals are fighting each other for the same money. The plan, as written, **cannot** deliver what it promises. Not because our leaders lack goodwill. Not because our people are lazy. **Because the plan is infeasible.**

That is a mathematical statement. Not politics, not opinion — no tribe, no party. And it was sitting inside a journal, where the people who write our national plans would never read it.

We have been writing national plans the way a lazy student writes a proof. He begins by saying: *"assume the result."*

*(Cue slide 11)*

## ▶ SLIDE 11 — THE NERVOUS SYSTEM

So let us talk about structures, because we are not the first people to attempt nation building. Working countries have structures. And I want to show you the mathematics hiding inside each one.

The structure is the body. **Mathematics is the nervous system.**

Number one, the national statistical system. This is the eye of the state — sampling, estimation, error bounds. Without it, government is a driver with a painted windscreen.

Number two, planning and budgeting. This is constrained optimisation and nothing else. Maximise welfare, subject to revenue, subject to debt, subject to time. Every budget in this world is a linear programme. Most of ours are written as wish lists.

> ⏱ **[CUT-8 — drop if short on time]**
> Number three, security. Graph theory. Network centrality. Because you do not defeat a network with a convoy. You defeat it by finding the nodes that hold it together, and removing them.

Number four, justice. Queueing theory. A case backlog is a queue, and in any queue where arrival rate is greater than service rate, that queue runs to infinity. So justice delayed is not merely justice denied. It is a queue that has become mathematically unstable, and it will never recover on its own.

> ⏱ **[CUT-3 — these last two can go if short on time]**
> Number five, public health. Epidemiological modelling. R-nought. Cold chain logistics. Every life saved in a pandemic is saved twice — once by a doctor, and once, earlier, by a differential equation.
>
> Number six, energy. Load balancing. Economic dispatch. Our grid collapses are not spiritual attack. They are unsolved optimisation problems, collapsing in public.

## ▶ SLIDE 12 — SIXTEEN SECTORS, ONE FOUNDATION

And it does not stop there. Look at this board.

Security. Peace. Justice. Governance. Health. Education. Planning. Logistics. Employment. Innovation. Agriculture. Food security. Entertainment. Energy. Anti-corruption. Finance.

Sixteen sectors. Sixteen! And underneath every single one of them, the same discipline.

Agriculture — we lose a serious portion of our harvest after we have already grown it. That is not a farming failure. That is a routing and storage failure. And routing is mathematics.

> ⏱ **[CUT-4 — drop if short on time]**
> Entertainment — yes, even entertainment. Why is the world now listening to Nigerian music? Part of it is our talent. But part of it is a recommendation algorithm on a streaming platform, and that algorithm is somebody's matrix factorisation. Our biggest cultural export is riding on mathematics we did not build and do not control.

Sixteen sectors, one foundation. That is not a coincidence.

**That is the definition of a foundational science.**

## ▶ SLIDE 13 — MATHEMATICS CATCHES LIARS

Let me give you one that will make you smile.

Corruption. We treat it as a moral problem, and it is. We treat it as a legal problem, and it is. But mathematically, corruption is an **anomaly detection problem**.

Look at this chart. This is Benford's Law. In genuine numerical data — real invoices, real populations, real river lengths — the leading digit one shows up about thirty percent of the time. Not eleven percent as you would expect. Thirty.

Now, when a man sits down to invent numbers, he does not know this. He spreads his digits nicely, because he thinks that is what random looks like. **Human beings are very, very bad at faking randomness.**

So an inflated contract carries a statistical fingerprint — visible before anybody confesses, before anybody testifies, before anybody is arrested.

Mathematics catches liars. And the sweet part is this: mathematics cannot be settled. You cannot call mathematics on the phone at night and beg it.

*(Cue slide 14)*

## ▶ SLIDE 14 — THE SLOW BLEEDING

Now, what happens to a nation that does not respect this?

Nothing dramatic. And that, my people, is exactly the danger.

When you ignore physics, a bridge falls down. Everybody sees it. Everybody talks about it. There is a panel of inquiry.

When you ignore mathematics, nothing collapses. Nothing at all. You simply become — quietly, expensively, permanently — **suboptimal**.

You build the road on the wrong corridor. You put the hospital where the people are not. You order too much of the drug that will expire, and too little of the one that would have saved somebody's mother. You fight last year's insurgency in this year's terrain. You produce graduates for jobs that no longer exist.

Nobody is arrested. No headline is written. The country simply runs at sixty percent of what it could have been — forever — and we give it a name. We call it "our peculiar Nigerian challenges."

**The cost of ignoring mathematics is never a crash. It is a slow bleeding.**

And there is a second consequence that is even more dangerous. When decisions are not made on evidence, that space does not stay empty. Something must fill it. And what fills it? Sentiment. Ethnicity. Political convenience. Who shouted last and loudest in the meeting.

Because **mathematics is the only argument in the room that has no tribe.**

Remove it, and you have removed the only referee that cannot be bribed.

> ⏱ **[CUT-6 — drop if short on time, but this is one of the best laughs in the speech]**
> But let me caution my own profession too, so we do not become proud. Bad mathematics is worse than no mathematics, because it carries an authority it has not earned. Remember the statistician who drowned crossing a river of average depth three feet. On average, he was fine. And remember always: correlation does not imply causation — although, I must admit, it does raise its eyebrow suggestively.

## ▶ SLIDE 15 — SIX THINGS WE MUST DO

Now, diagnosis without prescription is just fine complaining. So let me give you six things. Practical things.

**One.** Change what we reward in academic mathematics. Measure every department not only by papers published, but by problems solved for a real Nigerian institution. Adopt a ministry. Adopt a local government. Adopt a teaching hospital. Theory to impact is not a slogan for a banner. It should be a key performance indicator.

**Two.** Put mathematicians in the room where the problem is being **formulated** — not where the answer is being justified. Because in optimisation, the formulation determines the solution. Let politicians formulate and mathematicians only compute, and you will get a very efficient answer to the wrong question.

**Three.** Fix the pipeline at the source. Our children are not failing mathematics because they are dull. They are failing because we teach it as a ritual — memorise, reproduce, forget. A child who has never once used mathematics to answer a question he himself cared about has not been taught mathematics. He has been taught obedience.

**Four.** Build the data. Conduct the census. Digitise the registries. Open the data. Every clever thing we could build is currently starving at the input layer.

**Five.** Demand quantitative literacy from leadership. Not calculus — just the ability to look at a model and ask: what is the assumption here, and what is the error bar on that number? A leader who cannot ask that will be captured by whoever brings the most confident PowerPoint.

**Six.** Bridge to deployment. This is what we do at RAIN — take the theory and put it into a robot, an embedded system, a model running on a device somebody can hold in his hand. Mathematics that cannot be deployed is only a beautiful idea. Mathematics that has been deployed is a nation.

*(Cue slide 16)*

## ▶ SLIDE 16 — WHAT WE WOULD SEE

And let me tell you what it would actually look like, so that it does not remain abstract.

In **health**, drugs would arrive before they are needed instead of after. Outbreaks flattened by prediction instead of by panic. Specialists posted by disease burden, not by who lives close to the capital.

In **education**, teachers would be posted where the children actually are — not where the posting is convenient.

> ⏱ **[CUT-5 — drop if short on time]**
> In **agriculture**, planting decisions would follow yield models and satellite data, post-harvest losses would fall, and food prices would be settled by forecasting rather than by decree.

In **energy**, a grid that balances instead of a grid that collapses. And in **governance**, budgets that are feasible before they are signed, and inflated contracts flagged automatically before the money leaves the account.

And hear me well: **every single item on that list is already a solved problem somewhere else in the world today.** The mathematics exists. It is published. Much of it is open source and free.

We are not waiting for a discovery. **We are waiting for a decision.**

## ▶ SLIDE 17 — AI IS MATHEMATICS

Which brings me to where we are now. The future is not coming. The future has entered the room and sat down.

Artificial intelligence is already in our medicine, our agriculture, our banking, our classrooms, our security, our courts, our music, our elections.

And I want this hall to hear the next sentence very clearly, because it is the most important thing I will say this morning.

**Artificial intelligence is not a computing revolution. It is a mathematics revolution wearing a computing costume.**

Look at what is inside the machine. Linear algebra. Multivariable calculus. Probability. Optimisation. Information theory. That is it. That is all that is inside.

So **everybody sitting in this hall is already an AI researcher.** The subject the whole world is now pouring trillions of dollars into is your own subject. You were doing this thing before it became fashionable.

And that is why the opportunity is extraordinary. This country was locked out of the industrial revolution by capital. We were locked out of the digital revolution by infrastructure. But this one? Its main raw material is **mathematical talent** — and we have plenty of it, young, hungry, and sitting right here.

*(Cue slide 18)*

## ▶ SLIDE 18 — THE OTHER EDGE OF THE BLADE

But every opportunity carries its own danger. The bad people have the same mathematics we have. Very often, they have better funding.

Take steganography — the science of hiding information inside something that looks innocent. Recent research shows AI-generated images can now hide a payload in a way that is, by the information-theoretic definition, essentially perfect. Statistically you cannot tell that picture from any ordinary picture. And there are documented cases of malicious code hidden inside images posted openly on social media. So understand this: **a picture is no longer just a picture.** It can be a message. It can be a weapon.

Take synthetic media. A voice can be cloned from thirty seconds of audio. Thirty seconds! In a country where one false statement can start a fire in three states before lunchtime, a fabricated recording of a leader is not a technology problem. It is a national security problem.

> ⏱ **[CUT-7 — drop the post-quantum block if short on time]**
> Take encryption. Every classified communication, every bank transaction, every citizen record is protected by one mathematical assumption about how hard it is to factorise a large number. > Quantum computing is coming for that assumption, and other nations have already begun migrating to post-quantum cryptography. So let me ask this hall a serious question: **where is our own migration plan? Who in Nigeria is working on this?** Because if the answer is nobody, then our national secrets have an expiry date, and somebody else has set it.

So I say to you: we cannot be a nation that only **consumes** this mathematics. We must **audit** it, **build** it, and **defend against** it. That work is cryptography, adversarial machine learning, steganalysis, formal verification. It is not glamorous. But it is the difference between digital sovereignty and digital tenancy.

*(Cue slide 19)*

## ▶ SLIDE 19 — THE ONE VARIABLE WE CONTROL

So, distinguished ladies and gentlemen, let me bring it home.

We have talked about big things this morning. Poverty. Insecurity. A national plan that is mathematically infeasible. Quantum computing. Large problems — and it is easy to sit down and feel small.

But there is one variable in this whole system that you and I control completely.

**It is the number of people in this nation willing to carry rigour out of the classroom and apply it to the country.**

It is the professor who adopts a ministry. It is the postgraduate student who builds the model for the local government, not only for the journal. It is the young person at the back of this hall who will spend the next ten years making sure Nigeria's decisions can be defended with evidence.

And here is the sweetest part. For that variable you need no permission. No legislation. No budget line. Nobody has to sign anything for you.

**You can start on Monday morning.**

*(Cue slide 20)*

## ▶ SLIDE 20 — CLOSE

We began this morning by remembering people who had no computer, no calculator, no electricity — and who still gave the world the aeroplane, electricity, medicine, and the satellite.

We then agreed that today the limit is no longer the machine. Today the limit is us. Today it is a choice.

So let it be recorded that on this day, in this hall, a gathering of Nigerian mathematicians decided that *From Theory to Impact* was not a theme printed on a banner.

It was an instruction.

**Because a nation is not built by those who admire the problem. A nation is built by those who solve it.**

Thank you very much.
